Transcripts in a localization's native language are written as-is; no special formatting necessary

Lines in a different language are first transcribed in their respective language, preceded by the name of the language in brackets [like this], and followed by a translation in the localization's language in brackets
EXAMPLE : [Japanese] 立ち塞がる者は誰であれ斬り捨てる。 [I'll cut down anyone in my way.]

Slang, improper spelling, and stutters or interruptions should be used where necessary to reflect (notable) spoken inflection or other vocal quirks

Some specifics:
- Ellipses should be written with the ellipses character (…), not three periods (...)
- Use "yeah", not "yea"
- Use "ok", not "okay"

Errors in speech should also be faithfully transcribed. In these cases, a comment (started with two forward slashes) should be added above the incorrect line with "[sic];" and an explanation of what's incorrect
EXAMPLE : // [sic]; "detuvo" should be "detuve"

We're also tackling "closed captioning", which is a form of subtitling that makes special note to transcribe non-speech sounds played as part of the voice line, such as laughing or coughing, in brackets
Closed captions are descriptive but as short as possible, and written as noun phrases instead of verb phrases; for example, [taunting laugh] instead of [laughs tauntingly] or [mocking and goading laugh]
They're also always in lowercase, except for proper nouns
